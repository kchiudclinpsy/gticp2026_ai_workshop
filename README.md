# GTICP 2026 AI Workshop

A practical workshop for identifying meaningful challenges in clinical psychology training and using AI thoughtfully to develop and test solutions. Participants will move from a real pain point to a small, testable solution and use structured methods to stress-test it.

## 1. Learn how to spot a pain point worth solving

Start with a challenge experienced by trainees, staff, or the programme. Look beyond the first proposed solution and understand the problem itself.

- **Explain the impact:** Who is affected, and how?
- **Consider the opportunity:** If this problem were improved, would the benefits justify the time and effort required to solve it?
- **Test its significance:** Is the problem frequent enough, time-consuming enough, or impactful enough that solving it would create meaningful benefits for trainees, staff, or the programme?

A strong opportunity is grounded in people's actual experiences and has a plausible path to meaningful improvement.

## 2. Work with AI to co-develop a solution

Use AI as a collaborative tool to explore ideas, clarify requirements, and develop a possible solution. Keep people involved in decisions: AI can support the work, but it does not replace professional judgement, safeguarding, clinical governance, or accountability.

As you develop an idea, be clear about its intended purpose, users, data needs, and limitations. Avoid entering confidential or identifiable information into AI tools unless the tool has been approved for that use and the relevant information governance requirements have been met.

## 3. Stress-test your solution

Use **SHIELD** to blue-team your solution: identify safeguards and protections that should be in place. Use **PROBE** to red-team it: actively look for risks, unintended uses, and failure scenarios.

### Blue team: SHIELD

- **S – Safeguards:** What controls prevent harm or misuse?
- **H – Human oversight:** Who reviews outputs and remains accountable?
- **I – Information protection:** How will sensitive data be protected?
- **E – Evaluation:** How will accuracy, value, equity, and impact be tested?
- **L – Limits:** What must the AI not be used for?
- **D – Detection and response:** How will problems be identified, reported, and corrected?

### Red team: PROBE

- **P – Purpose:** Could the system be used beyond its intended purpose?
- **R – Risks:** What harm, misuse, or failure could occur?
- **O – Outputs:** Can inaccurate, biased, unsafe, or misleading outputs be produced?
- **B – Boundaries:** What happens at the system's limits or in unusual scenarios?
- **E – Exposure:** Could confidential information, vulnerabilities, or affected groups be exposed?

Use what you learn from both exercises to revise the solution, define when it should not be used, and decide what needs further testing or human review.
